01Course summary

For visual journalists, generative AI video can feel like a direct threat to core tenets of journalism. Can we use technology that generates synthetic reality to ethically tell a story? This 5-week, 1-credit module serves as an experimental lab to attempt to answer that question. We will examine the current landscape of non-fiction synthetic media through a critical lens, then move into hands-on production to determine if — and how — generative AI can serve ethical, fact-based reporting.

You'll experiment with the latest tools to create your journalism, whether that is animation or documentary re-creation without the need for actors. Perhaps it will be something completely new. The tools change by the week, so part of your job is finding the right tool for the story, not just learning one.

We'll organize our thinking around a spectrum. The further a technique moves from the camera, the heavier its ethical burden:

  1. EnhanceRestoring, upscaling, cleaning up real footage
  2. TransformStylizing or anonymizing real footage and sources
  3. ReconstructBuilding scenes from real photos, video and documents
  4. SynthesizeGenerating scenes, people and places from scratch
Closer to the cameraHeavier ethical burden

04Diversity, equity & inclusion

I've deliberately chosen topics, examples, and assignments that ask you to engage with a diversity of voices, perspectives, and lived experiences. In this space we stay open to learning and growth and commit to treating each other with courtesy and respect.

As journalists, you'll be expected to consider people and viewpoints you don't agree with. During discussions, respect each other's unique lived experiences and points of view, aim to be thoughtfully critical of the work and not the person, and always be kind.

07Assignments & grading

This is a seminar. You'll produce one short piece of journalism, moving through pitch → rough → final, and you'll fold my feedback on the rough into the final cut. Along the way you'll keep a production log — a running record of your sources, prompts, tools, and what's generated versus captured. With your final piece, you'll turn in a methodology statement explaining what you generated and why, the sourcing behind it, and your answer to the course's central question.

09Fabrication, disclosure & AI

This course is the exception to the rule you've heard in every other class: here, generative AI is the medium. That makes the rest of our standards matter more, not less.

  • Everything must be reported. Generated imagery has to be grounded in sourcing you can show — interviews, documents, photos, data. If you can't source it, don't generate it.
  • Everything must be disclosed. Your audience should never have to guess what's real, re-created or generated.
  • Keep your log honest. The production log is your record of what you did. Treat it like your notes.

If you have any doubts about something you're considering, come see me first. Better to figure out an ethically sound way to do something than to risk your credibility, reputation, and career. For the full Code of Ethics, see the Code of Ethics website.
